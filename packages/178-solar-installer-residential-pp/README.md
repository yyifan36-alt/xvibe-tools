## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How do you properly calculate a solar loan amount with a lender Dealer Fee?
A: Never multiply the cash price by (1 + Dealer Fee %). Because the solar lender deducts their fee percentage from the total gross loan amount, you must divide: Financed Loan Amount = Net Cash Price ÷ (1 - Dealer Fee Decimal). For example, a $28,000 cash deal with a 30% dealer fee is $28,000 ÷ 0.70 = $40,000.

### Q: What is a solar EPC redline and how are rep commissions calculated?
A: The Redline is the baseline price per watt (e.g., $2.15/W) that the EPC installer charges the sales organization to cover panels, inverters, racking, permits, and installation labor. Total commission equals (Net Sold Cash Price - (System Watts × Redline) - Project Adders).

### Q: What are the most common solar project adders that eat into rep commissions?
A: The five most expensive post-survey adders are 200A Main Panel Upgrades (MPU: $2,400–$3,200), Spanish/concrete tile roof mounts ($0.15–$0.25/W), steep-pitch/multi-array charges ($0.10–$0.20/W), structural rafter sistering ($1,200+), and ground-mount trenching ($25–$45/linear foot).

### Q: How are solar commissions typically split between a Setter and a Closer?
A: On self-gen deals, the closer keeps 100% of the net redline spread. On appointment-setter (canvasser/D2D) leads, standard splits range from 30% Setter / 70% Closer up to 50% / 50% for experienced setters who collect the full utility bill and shading photos.

### Q: What is the difference between M1, M2, and M3 payment milestones in residential solar?
A: M1 is an advance ($500–$1,500) paid once the site survey, engineering design, and HOA/permit submission are approved; M2 (typically 80% of remaining commission) is paid at physical 'glass on roof' installation completion; and M3 (final holdback) is released at utility PTO (Permission to Operate).
