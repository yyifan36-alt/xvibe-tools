## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why must dark garments be priced at +1 color count plus a flash-cure upcharge?
A: Dark cotton and polyester garments require a dedicated 110–156 mesh white underbase screen (or grey dye-blocker on poly) flashed under a 1,800°F quartz unit before top colors hit—adding a full screen setup and cutting press speed by 30%–35%.

### Q: What is the real internal shop cost to coat, burn, register, and reclaim one screen?
A: Between darkroom technician wages, emulsion, laser/CTS exposure, press taping, micro-registration, ink scrape, and dip-tank reclaim chemicals, each screen costs a shop $19 to $25—which is why waiving setup fees under 72 pieces destroys profit.

### Q: How do you price contract and custom embroidery per 1,000 stitches?
A: At an average running speed of 700–750 stitches per minute (including thread trims and hoop changes), a 6-head machine completes ~2.5 runs/hour on a 10,000-stitch logo. Charge a base 5,000–7,000 stitch minimum plus $0.85–$1.25 per additional 1,000 stitches.

### Q: Why should every screen printing quote build in a 2% to 3% blank spoilage allowance?
A: Even experienced automatic press operators encounter mill flaws, pallet adhesive pulls, or test-print misregistrations. Ordering 1–2 extra blanks per 72 pieces (or building a 2% spoilage factor into blank COGS) prevents short-shipping customers.
