## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How many students can fit on a martial arts or BJJ mat at one time?
A: For adult Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu and submission grappling with live rolling, budget 45 to 50 square feet of mat space per student (e.g., 30 grapplers require 1,350–1,500 sq.ft). For youth striking or karate forms, you can safely fit 1 student per 25 to 30 square feet.

### Q: What is the average monthly student churn rate for a BJJ or martial arts gym?
A: Industry-wide, adult martial arts gyms average 5.5% to 8.0% monthly churn (largely driven by new white belts quitting in the first 90 days), while structured kids' martial arts programs with stripe/belt milestones average 3.0% to 4.2% monthly churn.

### Q: How many active students does a martial arts school need to break even?
A: With a $4,500/month commercial lease, insurance, utilities, and $175 average monthly tuition, an owner-taught academy typically breaks even at 32 to 38 students, and hits $10,000+/month in net owner take-home around 115 to 130 active students.

### Q: Should a BJJ gym charge for belt promotions or include them in monthly tuition?
A: Most adult BJJ academies do not charge for adult belt promotions (monetizing instead via mandatory academy Gis, rashguards, and guest black-belt seminars), whereas youth karate/TKD and kids' BJJ programs commonly charge $45–$85 graduation event fees or build +$15/mo into tuition.

### Q: How much should a martial arts gym spend on instructor payroll?
A: Healthy academies keep total instructor and front-desk payroll between 18% and 26% of gross revenue, often compensating assistant coaches per class taught ($30–$50/class) plus a 50/50 split on 1-on-1 private lessons.
