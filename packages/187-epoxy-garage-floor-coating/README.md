## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How do you calculate exact epoxy and polyaspartic gallons using the 1,604 mil-spread formula?
A: One US gallon (231 cubic inches) of 100% solids coating covers 1,604 square feet at 1 mil (0.001 inch) wet-film thickness. Divide 1,604 by your target mil thickness: a 10-mil basecoat covers ~160 sq.ft./gal, and a 14-mil polyaspartic topcoat over scraped full-broadcast flake covers ~110–115 sq.ft./gal.

### Q: How many pounds of vinyl flake are needed for a full-broadcast rejection floor?
A: A true full-broadcast ('broadcast to rejection') 1/4-inch vinyl flake system consumes 0.13 to 0.16 lbs per square foot after scraping and vacuuming—meaning a 500 sq.ft. 2-car garage requires roughly two 40-lb boxes (75–80 lbs) of flake.

### Q: When must a concrete coating bid include a Moisture Vapor Barrier (MVB) primer?
A: Whenever calcium-chloride moisture testing exceeds 3 lbs/1,000 sq.ft./24 hrs (or relative humidity exceeds 75%–80%), standard basecoats can delaminate from hydrostatic pressure. An MVB epoxy primer requires an extra 8–10 mils (160–200 sq.ft./gal) and must be billed as a $0.75–$1.10/sq.ft. adder.

### Q: Why does a 1-day polyurea/polyaspartic system often yield higher net profit than 2-day epoxy?
