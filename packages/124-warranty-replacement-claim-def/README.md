## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why does a 3.5% product defect rate cost much more than 3.5% of my product COGS?
A: When you ship a free warranty replacement to a customer, you lose the replacement unit's landed COGS ($12–$35) PLUS outbound replacement postage ($7.50–$12.00) and your 3PL's pick-pack fulfillment fee ($2.50–$3.50). On a $14 COGS item with $9.00 reship fulfillment, each warranty replacement actually costs $23.00—making a 3.5% defect rate equal to 5.75% of COGS.

### Q: How much should an ecommerce brand set aside in a monthly warranty reserve fund?
A: Multiply your trailing 12-month warranty claim rate by the full-burden replacement cost (Landed Unit COGS + Outbound Replacement Shipping + 3PL Pick-Pack Fee) for every unit sold. Holding this exact dollar amount ($0.75–$2.10 per order) in a dedicated reserve account prevents Q1 post-holiday warranty claims from distorting your monthly P&L.

### Q: How do I recover excess defect costs from my overseas factory?
A: Most manufacturing contracts include a 1% to 2% defective allowance (often provided as free spare units on the PO). When a bad component batch pushes observed warranty claims to 4.5% or 6%, you should charge back both the excess unit FOB cost and domestic reship expenses as a credit against your next production deposit.

### Q: Is it more profitable to self-insure extended warranties or use a third-party app like Extend or Mulberry?
A: For most DTC brands with landed COGS under $40 and defect rates below 5%, self-insuring a 1-year or 2-year accidental/extended replacement plan is dramatically more profitable. Third-party warranty providers typically keep 60% to 75% of the plan price, whereas self-insuring lets you keep 100% of the $9.99–$19.99 upsell revenue while funding replacements at your wholesale landed cost.
