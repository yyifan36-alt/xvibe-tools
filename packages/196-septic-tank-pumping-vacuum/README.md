## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why should septic tank pumping always be priced up to 1,000 gallons with a per-gallon overage?
A: Municipal wastewater treatment plants (WWTPs) bill septage by the gallon ($0.08–$0.16/gal), and a 1,500-gallon tank takes up half of a 3,000-gal vacuum truck (forcing an early dump run). Capping base pricing at 1,000 gallons and billing $0.22–$0.30/gal above 1,000 gallons protects route margins.

### Q: How much profit does a 24-inch septic tank riser and effluent filter upsell add per stop?
A: A 24-inch Tuf-Tite or Polylok concrete adapter ring, 12-inch riser section, Polyurethane adhesive, and heavy-duty lid cost ~$78–$95 wholesale. Installed while the hole is already open for $295–$375 (plus $95 for an outlet tee effluent filter), it adds $220–$280 in net profit.

### Q: How should we price locating and hand-digging buried concrete septic lids?
A: Include only ground-level or <6-inch exposed lids in your base pump rate. For lids buried 6 to 24+ inches in clay or roots, charge $50–$75 per lid (or $85/hour digging labor)—and credit $50 of that digging fee if the homeowner approves a permanent grade-level riser install.
