## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why do independent pharmacies lose money filling brand-name GLP-1 prescriptions (Ozempic, Mounjaro, Wegovy)?
A: Many commercial and Medicare Part D PBM contracts reimburse brand medications at AWP minus 24% to 26.5%, whereas wholesale acquisition cost (WAC minus ~3.5% prompt-pay discount) equals roughly AWP minus 19.6%. That 5%–6% gap translates to a -$30 to -$55 cash loss on every $950 pen dispensed.

### Q: How do brand GLP-1 fills hurt my wholesaler generic rebates?
A: Wholesaler generic rebate matrices (McKesson OneStop, Cardinal SOURCE, Cencora PRxO) tier your generic rebate percentage based on your Generic Compliance Ratio (Generic Purchases ÷ Total Rx Purchases). Adding $150,000/month in brand GLP-1 purchases dilutes your GCR percentage and can trigger a 2%–4% rebate drop across all generics.

### Q: What happened to retroactive pharmacy DIR fees after the 2024 CMS reform?
A: CMS eliminated retroactive Part D pharmacy DIR clawbacks by moving price concessions to the Point of Sale (POS)—resulting in lower upfront AWP/NADAC reimbursement rates at adjudication, while commercial PBMs continue to enforce Generic Effective Rate (GER) and Brand Effective Rate (BER) contract true-ups.

### Q: What is the true cost to dispense a single prescription in an independent pharmacy?
A: When you divide total pharmacy operating overhead (pharmacist and technician salaries, vials/caps, PMS software, E&O insurance, and rent) by annual scripts filled, NCPA Digest data places average cost-to-dispense between $11.80 and $13.50 per prescription.

### Q: How does a Cost-Plus Cash model help independent pharmacies survive PBM compression?
A: By pricing cash generics at actual NADAC acquisition cost plus a transparent $10–$15 dispensing fee (plus 15% on tiers), pharmacies eliminate PBM switch fees, reject predatory discount-card ($9/claim) clawbacks, and guarantee a positive net contribution on every fill.
