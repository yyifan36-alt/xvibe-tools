## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: What is a fair festival pitch fee or revenue split for a food truck?
A: Healthy street fairs and festivals charge either a flat $150–$400 pitch fee or a 10%–15% revenue share. Once an organizer demands 20%–25% of gross sales plus a base booth fee, you must raise festival menu prices by 15%–20% to protect margin.

### Q: How much does a commercial commissary kitchen cost per month for a food truck?
A: Licensed food truck commissaries typically charge $750 to $1,500 per month depending on overnight truck parking with 30A/50A shore power, walk-in cooler shelf space, potable water fill, and greywater/grease trap dumping.

### Q: How do I calculate the maximum sales my food truck can do in one shift?
A: Multiply your peak hourly ticket capacity (e.g., 45 tickets/hour at 80 seconds per order) by the true peak rush hours (usually 3 to 4 hours, not the full 8-hour festival duration) and multiply by your average ticket ($17).

### Q: How much propane and generator fuel does a food truck burn per day?
A: A standard hot-line truck running two fryers and a flat-top griddle uses roughly 35 to 60 lbs of propane ($35–$55) plus 4 to 6 gallons of gasoline ($16–$24) for a 7000W–9500W inverter generator per full shift.

### Q: Why is corporate catering more profitable than public food truck festivals?
A: Corporate drop-off or guaranteed-minimum truck catering eliminates the 20% festival pitch fee and eliminates unpurchased food prep spoilage—knowing exact headcount in advance cuts food waste from 9% to nearly 0%.
