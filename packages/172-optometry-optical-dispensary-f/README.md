## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why do optometry clinics lose money on $150+ vision insurance frame allowances?
A: Managed vision plans reimburse the practice a fixed wholesale frame allowance ($52–$68) plus a dispensing fee, AND require the practice to give the patient a 20% discount on any amount over the allowance. Putting a $80 wholesale designer frame into a vision plan slot results in a net loss.

### Q: What is a healthy frame board turn rate for an independent optical dispensary?
A: High-performing optical dispensaries turn each frame peg 2.5 to 3.5 times per year. To calculate your required board size, multiply your annual frames sold by 1, then divide by 3.0 (e.g., 1,800 frames sold/yr ÷ 3.0 = 600 frame board pegs).

### Q: What should our wholesale frame markup multiplier be?
A: For frames acquired at $20–$38 wholesale (independent or private-label collections), a 3.2x to 3.8x markup sets retail at $135–$165—allowing patients to stay within their vision plan allowance while the clinic nets $30+ on the frame alone.

### Q: When does buying an in-house patternless lens edger pay off for an optometry practice?
A: A $26,000–$32,000 in-house edger/tracer system typically pays for itself in 9 to 14 months once a practice cuts at least 35 to 45 private-pay, VSP in-office finishing, or 2nd-pair Single Vision jobs per month using $16–$22 finished AR stock lenses.

### Q: How should opticians be incentivized without encouraging low-margin discounts?
A: Replace flat per-job bonuses with tiered net-margin spiffs: $10 for basic vision-plan single vision, $25 for premium digital progressive + Tier-3 AR upgrades, and $35 for uncovered private-pay 2nd-pair Rx sunglasses or computer glasses.
