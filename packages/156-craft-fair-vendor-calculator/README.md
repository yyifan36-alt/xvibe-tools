## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why is dividing Booth Fee by Item Price a broken way to calculate craft fair breakeven?
A: If your booth fee is $200 and you sell $25 candles that cost $8 in wax, jar, and fragrance oil plus $0.80 in Square tap fees, you don't break even at 8 candles ($200). Your net contribution per candle is only $16.20—and once you add $45 in gas, parking, and single-event insurance, you must sell 16 candles ($400) just to hit $0.00 before paying yourself a penny for labor.

### Q: What is the '5x to 7x Booth Fee Rule' used by full-time craft show vendors?
A: Experienced makers target gross booth sales of at least 5x to 7x the booth fee (e.g., $1,250 to $1,750 in sales on a $250 booth). At a 5x multiple, roughly 20% goes to the booth and travel, 28% goes to materials and POS fees, and ~52% remains to pay your production and weekend booth hours.

### Q: How much inventory should I bring to a weekend craft fair or pop-up market?
A: Bring 2.5x to 3.5x your target sales goal in retail value. If your goal is $1,500, pack $4,000+ of inventory structured as a pyramid: 25% low-friction impulse items ($10–$18), 55% core profit drivers ($28–$45), and 20% high-ticket showstoppers ($75+) that draw eyes to your tent.

### Q: How do bundling ('3 for $40') and tax-included round pricing boost booth throughput?
A: During peak 11:00 AM–2:00 PM foot traffic, fumbling with coins or waiting for a slow cell-tower card reader kills line velocity. Pricing in clean $5 increments (tax included for cash) and offering a '1 for $15 / 3 for $40' bundle doubles units per transaction while paying Square's fixed $0.15 fee only once.

### Q: What hidden craft fair expenses do first-time vendors forget to amortize?
A: Non-refundable $25–$50 jury application fees, $49 ACT single-event liability insurance, municipal temporary seller permits, 40-lb canopy leg weights, hotel/meals on 2-day shows, and IRS mileage ($0.67/mile round trip).
