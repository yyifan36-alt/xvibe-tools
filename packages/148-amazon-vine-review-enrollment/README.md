## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How do Amazon Vine's three enrollment fee tiers ($0, $75, and $200) work?
A: Amazon charges $0 enrollment fee when you enroll 1–2 units per parent ASIN, $75 when you enroll 3–10 units, and $200 when you enroll 11–30 units. However, you still pay the manufacturing COGS and standard FBA fulfillment fee on every unit claimed by a Vine Voice.

### Q: When is the $75 (10-unit) Vine tier more profitable than the $200 (30-unit) tier?
A: For products with landed COGS + FBA fees above $18, jumping from 10 units to 30 units adds $125 in Vine fees plus 20 extra giveaway units ($360+), while the marginal conversion lift from 8 reviews to 23 reviews is much smaller than the initial jump from 0 to 8 reviews.

### Q: What percentage of claimed Amazon Vine units actually result in a published review?
A: Across most FBA categories, 75% to 82% of claimed Vine units result in a published review within 30 days. Factoring in the 18%–25% non-review rate is critical to calculating your true dollar cost per live review.

### Q: When does Amazon charge the $75 or $200 Vine enrollment fee?
A: Amazon bills the Vine enrollment fee 7 days after the first Vine review is published. If zero reviews are published within 90 days of enrollment, no Vine enrollment fee is charged—though you still lose the COGS and FBA fee on claimed units.

### Q: Why shouldn't I turn on aggressive Amazon PPC before my first 5 Vine reviews land?
A: Shoppers rarely buy a 0-review listing at full price. Running $100/day in PPC at a 0-review 4% conversion rate burns budget and tanks your honeymoon conversion velocity metric; waiting until 5–8 Vine reviews post often doubles PPC conversion.

### Q: Does VinePayback Studio upload our unlaunched ASINs or supplier COGS to any server?
A: Never. All Vine tier comparisons, FBA giveaway calculations, and CSV exports run 100% locally in your browser RAM.
