## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How many drinks per guest should a wedding venue budget for a 5-hour open bar?
A: Industry standard banquet math budgets 1.5 drinks per guest during the first hour (cocktail hour) and 1.0 drink per guest for each subsequent reception hour—totaling 5.5 to 6.0 drinks per guest across a 5-hour open bar.

### Q: What is a profitable pour cost percentage for a wedding venue bar package?
A: Unlike street bars that run 20%–24% pour cost, pre-paid wedding open-bar packages ($45–$65/guest) should achieve a 16% to 21% blended pour cost across house/call spirits, draft/bottled beer, and case-discounted wine.

### Q: How much should a BYOB wedding venue charge for corkage and bar setups?
A: Venues where the client supplies the alcohol should charge $16 to $25 per guest for a 'Bar Mixer & Glassware Package' covering TABC/TIPS bartenders, 2 lbs of ice per guest, citrus garnishes, sodas/juices, cups/glassware, and liquor liability insurance.

### Q: Why does a 22% mandatory service charge cost the venue extra payroll tax?
A: The IRS classifies mandatory banquet service charges (e.g., 20%–24% admin/service fee) as gross receipts rather than voluntary tips, meaning any portion paid out to banquet captains and servers incurs employer FICA/Medicare and workers' comp burden.

### Q: How should wedding venues price overtime hours to prevent late vendor load-outs?
A: Separate 'Reception Party Overtime' ($750–$1,200/hr including bar/security) from 'Vendor Strike/Load-Out Overtime' ($350–$500/hr after the contracted 60-minute teardown window), backed by a refundable security deposit.
