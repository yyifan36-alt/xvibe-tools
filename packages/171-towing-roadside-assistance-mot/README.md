## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: Why do towing companies lose money on motor club (AAA / Agero / Honk) calls?
A: Contracted motor club rates ($48–$65 base hook fee with 5 to 10 free loaded miles) assume the truck is already 2 miles away. When a dispatcher accepts a call 14 miles away, the 28 round-trip unpaid deadhead miles plus 30% driver commission exceed the entire payout.

### Q: What is the true cost per mile to operate a commercial flatbed or wheel-lift tow truck?
A: When you include 8 to 9 MPG diesel fuel, commercial towing insurance ($12,000–$18,000/yr per truck), hydraulic winch/bed maintenance, tires, and truck note depreciation over 60,000 annual miles, true operating cost before driver pay is $1.35 to $1.65 per mile.

### Q: How should a towing company pay tow truck drivers?
A: Most profitable light-duty fleets pay 25% to 30% commission on gross towing and roadside charges (or an hourly base + 15% commission), while excluding or paying a reduced 5%–10% bounty on multi-day impound lot storage fees.

### Q: How much does an impound storage lot add to a towing company's profit?
A: Police rotation accident tows and Private Property Impounds (PPI) that enter your secure storage yard average 4 to 9 days of storage at $50–$85/day (paid by auto insurers via Copart/IAA on collision total losses), often doubling net profit per call.

### Q: What is a fair retail cash towing rate for light-duty vehicles?
A: Standard retail cash rates for light-duty flatbed or wheel-lift towing range from $115 to $150 base hook fee plus $4.00 to $6.00 per loaded mile, with +$65 to +$95 add-ons for GoJak skates, dollies, off-road winching, or after-hours service.
