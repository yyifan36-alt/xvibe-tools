## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How much weight should I include in a 15-yard or 20-yard dumpster rental?
A: Most profitable independent haulers include 1.5 tons in a 15-yard dumpster, 2.0 tons in a 20-yard dumpster, and 3.0 tons in a 30-yard dumpster—charging $75 to $105 per additional prorated ton based on the landfill scale ticket.

### Q: What is a healthy net profit margin per pull in the roll-off dumpster business?
A: After deducting municipal landfill or transfer-station tipping fees ($50–$85/ton), hook-lift/cable truck diesel, CDL/non-CDL driver labor, and credit card processing, operators should target $175 to $260+ in net profit per pull (40%–48% net margin).

### Q: How many times per month should a roll-off dumpster container turn?
A: By enforcing a 7-day standard residential rental period (with a $15–$20/day daily extension fee beginning on Day 8) alongside fast 2-to-3 day roofer and contractor swaps, a well-managed fleet averages 3.5 to 4.5 pulls per container per month.

### Q: Why should my overweight tonnage charge be higher than the landfill tipping fee?
A: If your local transfer station charges $65/ton, charging customers $65/ton at cost loses money because heavy loads increase brake/tire wear, reduce hook-lift fuel economy, incur 2.9% credit card fees on the overage charge, and risk DOT axle-weight fines. Charge at least a +$25 to +$35/ton markup.

### Q: How do I prevent customers from disputing overweight dumpster charges?
A: Include an explicit card-on-file overweight authorization clause in your digital rental agreement, cap prohibited heavy materials (wet dirt, concrete, brick) to 10-yard low-boy cans only, and automatically text/email a photo of the timestamped certified landfill scale ticket with the receipt.
