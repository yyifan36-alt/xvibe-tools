## Frequently Asked Questions (Direct Citation Q&A)
### Q: How is midpoint arc price elasticity of demand (PED) calculated?
A: Midpoint arc elasticity divides the percentage change in quantity demanded by the percentage change in price using the average of the starting and ending values as the base: ((Q2 - Q1) / ((Q1 + Q2) / 2)) divided by ((P2 - P1) / ((P1 + P2) / 2)). This eliminates directional bias whether you test a price increase or a discount.

### Q: Why did my net profit drop even though my 20% off promo increased unit sales by 50%?
A: Because a discount comes directly out of your unit contribution margin. If your baseline gross margin is 40%, a 20% price cut slices your dollar profit per unit in half—meaning you must double unit sales (+100% volume lift) just to earn the same total profit dollars.

### Q: How do I know if I should raise prices instead of discounting?
A: When a SKU has inelastic demand (|PED| < 1.0) or when its elasticity magnitude is smaller than the reciprocal of your contribution margin ratio, a 10% to 15% price increase loses fewer unit sales than the extra margin gained per unit, resulting in higher net profit.

### Q: How does chained B2B trade discount math (such as 20/10/5) work?
A: Chained trade discounts are applied sequentially to the remaining balance, not added together. A 20/10/5 chain equals 1 - (0.80 * 0.90 * 0.95) = 31.6% net equivalent discount, not 35%. Our calculator computes the exact net invoice price and margin floor.

A: No. ElasticityMargin Studio runs 100% locally inside your browser. Your supplier COGS, historical unit volumes, and wholesale price tiers never leave your device.
